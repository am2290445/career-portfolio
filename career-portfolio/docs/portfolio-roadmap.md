# Portfolio roadmap

See the [Portfolio Plan](/plan) for the two website approaches, user stories, checks, and professional-material destinations for this course milestone.

These are project ideas that connect Business and Information Systems study to AI-related work. They are **plans**, not completed projects or requirements from an employer.

## Project 1 — Map a business process

Write down one simple process, such as handling an online order or responding to a customer request.

- **Practice:** requirements, process steps, users, and information flow.
- **Course connection:** IS 390 — Requirements Analysis and Systems Design; IS 455 — IS Management and Business Processes.
- **Evidence to save:** a one-page process map, the business question, and a short explanation of where information systems help.

## Project 2 — Find a pattern in sample data

Use public or sample data to answer a question such as “Which products sold most each month?”

- **Practice:** data cleaning, spreadsheets or SQL, analysis, and visualization.
- **Course connection:** IS 331 — Database Design, Management and Applications; MGMT 216 — Business Data Analytics.
- **Evidence to save:** a cleaned sample dataset, query or spreadsheet, chart, and a short plain-language summary.

## Project 3 — Make the result easy to use

Turn the finding into a small web page or simple app.

- **Practice:** web development, communication, and testing.
- **Course connection:** IS 218 — Building Web Applications.
- **Evidence to save:** working page, source code, README, and a note about how you checked it.

## Optional AI step

Ask an AI tool to summarize the sample data or draft a response for a clearly defined task. Compare its answer with the source data. Record one correct result, one error or limitation, and what a person should review.

**Use only public or sample information. Do not put private customer or personal data into an AI tool.**

## Keep the work honest

For each project, label what is planned and what is finished. A future test is not a test result. When finished, say what you built, what you checked, and what you learned.
