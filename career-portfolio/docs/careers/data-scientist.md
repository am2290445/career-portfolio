# Data Scientist

<span class="eyebrow">CAREER FILE 05 / STATISTICS & MODELING</span>

## Job overview

A Data Scientist uses statistics, programming, and domain knowledge to investigate complex questions and build models that support prediction, experimentation, or decision-making.

## Job description / responsibilities

- Translate business questions into analytical problems.
- Explore, clean, and analyze data.
- Apply statistical methods and machine learning where appropriate.
- Develop and evaluate predictive or explanatory models.
- Communicate uncertainty, limitations, and findings.
- Work with engineers to make analytical outputs usable in business processes.

## Skills and tools

Python or R, SQL, statistics, probability, experimental design, data visualization, machine learning fundamentals, and communication. Tools can include pandas, scikit-learn, Jupyter, and cloud data platforms.

## Education and preparation

Employers vary widely. Some request a bachelor's degree; research-heavy roles may prefer graduate study in statistics, computer science, mathematics, or a related field. A strong foundation in statistics, programming, and domain knowledge is important.

## How it uses data analytics

Data science often combines descriptive, diagnostic, predictive, and sometimes prescriptive analysis. Model evaluation should include baselines, validation, error analysis, and awareness of bias and data leakage.

## How the role can help a business grow

- Forecast demand and support inventory planning.
- Identify customer segments and opportunities for personalization.
- Estimate risk and detect patterns for investigation.
- Evaluate experiments and measure whether product changes work.
- Improve resource allocation when predictions are reliable and actionable.

**Illustrative business example:** A subscription business wants to understand cancellations. A Data Scientist studies historical behavior, evaluates a churn model against a simple baseline, and explains which signals are associated with risk. The business can test outreach strategies and measure retention. Prediction is not proof of causation.

## Portfolio project

Create a transparent prediction project with a documented dataset, exploratory analysis, train/test split, baseline, evaluation metrics, limitations, and a nontechnical summary. Explain how a decision-maker might use the result.

## Career development

Senior Data Scientist, Applied Scientist, Decision Scientist, Data Science Manager, or specialized research roles.
