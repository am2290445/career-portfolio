My résumé lists Python, Git/GitHub, database fundamentals, and website development; those are self-reported starting points, not expert-level claims. The portfolio should connect a skill to a class or project artifact when one is available.

**Portfolio promise:** Make my developing interests, course work, and next steps easy to understand and check.

**Secondary possible reader:** A class reviewer or future teammate who wants to see how I connect a business need to an information-system or data solution. This is a planning hypothesis, not a researched customer finding.

## Two concise site approaches

Both options use the same audience, promise, career facts, projects, and calls to action. The options change what appears first so I can compare which path makes the evidence easiest to find.

### Option A — Career map first

Open with my Business and Information Systems direction, then show the current opportunity types and a short employer-skill map. Follow with course-project evidence, individual contributions, and professional materials. This option makes my career reasoning visible before asking the reader to evaluate my current work.

### Option B — Project evidence first

Open with the same short introduction, then show course projects, my specific contribution, and the checks I performed. Follow with current opportunity research and the employer-skill map, then professional materials. This option lets a recruiter inspect examples sooner.

**Proposed starting point:** Option B may fit a recruiter who needs evidence quickly; Option A may better explain why I am pursuing this work. I will keep the promise, brand, and evidence the same and use a classmate walkthrough to choose. I have not tested either option yet, so this is a proposal rather than a result.

## Brand choices and honest persuasion

- **Primary archetype:** Explorer — curiosity, discovery, and learning.
- **Supporting archetype:** Creator — turning ideas into useful projects.
- **Visual direction:** Postmodernism, as recorded in the existing portfolio README: expressive type, layered cards, collage-like composition, and unexpected color combinations, balanced with clear reading order and keyboard access.
- **Voice:** Curious, concrete, and honest. Label coursework as coursework and planned skills as learning goals.
- **Authority application:** Link to actual project repositories and explain my own contribution, choices, tools, and checks. The evidence may help a reader judge my current work; it does not claim professional employment or a hiring outcome.
- **Reciprocity application:** Offer a short, useful role-to-skill map before a contact request—for example, “role → skill to practice → project evidence to build.” This offers a practical starting point; it does not prove that a recruiter will respond.
